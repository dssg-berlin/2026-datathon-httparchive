Empirical Insights & Comparative Analysis (2025 vs. 2026)

Our empirical analysis based on HTTP Archive datasets challenges the generalized panic regarding the widespread risk of PHP-related vulnerabilities, revealing a more nuanced and encouraging security posture across the web ecosystem:

    Overall PHP Adoption Trend: The global share of websites utilizing PHP has experienced a notable decline. While in 2025 PHP adoption stood stable at approximately 52.08% for mobile and 51.84% for desktop, the 2026 data shows a significant drop to 47.31% (mobile) and 47.90% (desktop), indicating a gradual migration towards alternative backend technologies.

    Vulnerability Concentration (EOL Versions): Despite the overall drop in adoption, security risks remain heavily concentrated around outdated and End-of-Life (EOL) versions. Specifically, versions such as PHP 7.4.33 continue to maintain an unexpectedly high exposure across millions of origins, highlighting a persistent lag in software maintenance and version upgrading among web administrators.

    Client Discrepancies: Mobile and desktop environments show consistent structural patterns in version distribution, though mobile platforms consistently host a higher absolute volume of exposed legacy endpoints.
